# How Ghanaian Churches Are Using SMS to Boost Sunday Attendance and Keep Members Connected

Every church admin knows the feeling: you post the event flyer on the church WhatsApp status, mention it twice from the pulpit, and half the congregation still shows up asking "wait, was there something happening today?" It's not that members don't care — it's that between work, family, and life, an announcement made once on a Sunday morning is easy to miss by Wednesday.

Social media and email have the same problem for churches that they have for businesses: they only reach the members who happen to be checking that platform, at that moment. A text message doesn't have that problem. It arrives on the one device almost every member checks throughout the day, whether or not they're active on Facebook or reading email.

Here's how churches across Ghana are putting that to work.

## Service and event reminders that actually land

A short reminder sent the evening before or the morning of a service does something a pulpit announcement can't: it reaches members who weren't in church last week to hear it. The same goes for midweek programs, revivals, and one-off events — a reminder timed close to the event consistently does more for attendance than an announcement made days in advance and then forgotten.

## Group messaging by ministry, not the whole congregation

Not every message is for everyone. Choir rehearsal changes, usher scheduling, youth ministry outings — sending these to your entire contact list trains members to tune out your texts, because most of what arrives isn't relevant to them. Grouping contacts by ministry, department, or small group means the youth pastor can message the youth ministry directly, without the rest of the congregation getting a text that doesn't concern them. It keeps every message relevant, which keeps members actually reading them.

## Birthdays and anniversaries, without anyone having to remember

Few things make a member feel seen like a birthday message from their church — and few things are easier to let slip when it's one more task for an already-stretched admin team. Automated birthday and anniversary messages solve this quietly: add a birthdate once, and the wish goes out every year without anyone having to check a calendar. It's a small thing that adds up to members feeling genuinely remembered, not managed.

## Fundraising and giving reminders, timed right

Building funds, harvest, and outreach drives all depend on timely reminders — not a single announcement weeks before the deadline, but a nudge close enough to the date that it's still top of mind. A scheduled SMS a few days before a giving deadline tends to do far more than a mention from the pulpit a month earlier.

## Reaching the members email and social media miss

Every congregation has members who don't check email regularly and aren't on the church's social media — often older members, or simply people who don't engage with those platforms day to day. SMS reaches them the same way it reaches everyone else: directly, on the device they already check.

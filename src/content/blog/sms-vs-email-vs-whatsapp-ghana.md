# SMS vs Email vs WhatsApp: What Actually Gets Opened in Ghana

Most businesses and churches in Ghana are already using two or three of these channels without thinking much about which one is right for which message. A promotion goes out on WhatsApp status, a receipt gets emailed, an appointment reminder... doesn't get sent at all, because nobody decided who owns that job. The result is inconsistent communication and messages that land with the wrong audience, or don't land at all.

None of these three channels is universally "best." Each one is good at something specific — the trick is matching the channel to what the message actually needs to do.

## Email: good for records, weak for urgency

Email is built for things people don't need to act on immediately: receipts, invoices, newsletters, longer updates with detail to spare. It's cheap to send at volume and gives you room to write more than a couple of lines.

Where it falls short is timing. Email sits in an inbox until someone opens their mail app — which, for a lot of people, is not something that happens daily. A same-day flash sale or a "your appointment is in one hour" reminder loses most of its value if it's read two days late, or not at all.

**Best for:** receipts, invoices, newsletters, anything with no time pressure.

## WhatsApp: good for conversations, weak for guaranteed reach

WhatsApp is where a lot of everyday business communication in Ghana already happens, and for one-on-one conversations — answering a customer question, confirming an order back and forth — it's hard to beat. It's personal, it's fast, and replies come naturally.

The catch is reach. WhatsApp messaging at scale (broadcast lists, WhatsApp Business API) depends on the recipient having WhatsApp installed, having your number saved in some cases, and having a working internet connection at the moment you send. For a message that absolutely needs to reach every customer or member — not just the ones with a stable data connection at that moment — that's a real gap.

**Best for:** two-way conversations, customer support, order confirmations where a reply is expected.

## SMS: good for anything time-sensitive, works without data

SMS doesn't need an app, a data connection, or a saved contact. It arrives on any phone, smart or basic, the moment it's sent — which is exactly why it's the channel for anything where timing matters: appointment reminders, flash sales, service reminders, delivery updates, payment reminders. Read rates on SMS are consistently higher than email, and unlike WhatsApp, there's no dependency on internet access at the moment of delivery.

Its limits are the flip side of its strength: 160 characters keeps messages short, and it's not the place for a detailed newsletter or a back-and-forth conversation.

**Best for:** appointment reminders, promotions with a deadline, service announcements, delivery and order updates, anything where "the customer needs to see this today" matters.

## Using all three, deliberately

The businesses and churches that communicate best in Ghana usually aren't picking one channel — they're assigning each one a job. Email for receipts and longer updates. WhatsApp for direct, two-way conversations. SMS for anything time-sensitive that needs to reach everyone, not just the people who happen to be online. Getting that split right does more for how "on top of it" your communication feels than switching platforms ever will.
